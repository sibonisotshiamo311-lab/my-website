Geyser parts we fit

These are the parts that heat your water, control it and keep your geyser safe. Not sure which one has failed? Call us and we will find the fault.

Heating

  • Heating elements (2kW to 4kW): heat the water inside the cylinder. Spiral and boss types, in standard or hard-water Incoloy.
  • Thermostats (20A clip-in or clip-on): keep the water at the set temperature and cut the power when it is reached. Common sizes are 11 inch (275mm).

Safety

  • Pressure control and relief valves (400kPa or 600kPa): control the cold water pressure coming in and prevent over-pressure.
  • Temperature and pressure (T&P) valve (600kPa): releases hot water or steam if the temperature or pressure in the tank goes past safe limits.
  • Vacuum breakers (20mm or 22mm): fitted upright on the pipes so water cannot be siphoned out of the tank when the mains supply drops.
  • Drip trays and pipe lagging: trays (for example 1600 x 600mm for horizontal geysers) catch leaks, and R1 foam insulation lags the hot water pipes. Both are required by building regulations.

Protection and fittings

  • Magnesium (sacrificial) anodes: attract corrosive minerals in the water so the steel tank does not rust.
  • Drain cocks (22mm male or female): let the tank be drained for maintenance or when an element is replaced.
  • Flanges and gaskets: seal the side-plate opening where the element and thermostat are fitted. For example Ariston Axios 5-hole flanges and Kwikot gaskets.

Part sizes and ratings depend on your geyser's brand and model. Tell us whether yours is a Kwikot, Ariston or another make, and we will bring the right part.

Repair or replace your geyser?

Ask yourself these five questions before you decide.

How old is the geyser?

Check the manufacture date on the serial number or data plate rather than guessing. Storage geysers usually last 8 to 12 years. Past about 10 years, repairs often only delay a failure.

Is this the first fault, or the third?

One failed element or thermostat on a fairly new geyser is a normal, affordable repair. A second or third breakdown within 18 months often means the tank is corroded or failing.

Is the tank leaking, or just a valve or fitting?

Water around a geyser does not always mean a broken tank. It can come from a loose pipe connection, a faulty pressure relief valve or the drip tray. If the inner tank has cracked or burst, it must be replaced.

What is the water quality and service history?

Has the sacrificial anode been replaced every 3 to 4 years? In areas with hard water or sediment, a geyser that was never serviced may already be scaled and rusted inside.

Can the symptom be fixed instead?

Rusty water, rumbling or popping noises and fast loss of hot water can sometimes be fixed by flushing sediment, or replacing an element or anode, as long as the tank itself is sound.

Tell us your geyser's age and the problem, such as a leak, noise or no hot water, and we will tell you whether repair or replacement makes sense.

Industry standards and accreditations

What to check before you let anyone work on your geyser.

Registered plumbers

Look for technicians registered with the PIRB (Plumbing Industry Registration Board) and members of IOPSA (Institute of Plumbing South Africa).

Approved materials

Make sure the materials used are SABS or SANS compliant. This matters for your insurance and for your compliance certificate (CoC).
